## 2. Goals, non-goals, and anti-goals

Goals:

1. One module says when two points are the same and when two directions are parallel. Its values are derived, not chosen by feel.
2. Plan geometry is derived once per floor per change. Room derivation, the plan, the 3D view, and export read the same object.
3. The inner form of that geometry is chosen on measured evidence: the trial that ADR-0097 asked for runs before any consumer moves.
4. Every reader of a wall's centerline goes through one accessor, and no file outside the kernel reads a wall's end points.
5. Input that used to break the derivation gives a defined result: a wall along another wall, a wall of no length, walls that nearly cross.
6. A room keeps its identity and its user data when the walls around it are edited.
7. A wall can be an arc. The plan, the 3D view, room areas, and export all show the same arc.
8. A length reads back as it was typed.
9. Property-based tests hold the derivation to its rules.

Non-goals:

- The rest of #80: openings in an arc wall, arcs of more than a half circle, a wall made of several curves, curve kinds other than a circular arc, and dimensions on an arc. They get the design round the owner has already scheduled.
- A constraint solver or driving dimensions (#693).
- Typed length entry in the wall tool. Section 11 explains why this matters for one of the umbrella's conditions.
- Moving the snapping and hit-testing modules out of `editor/`. The kernel replaces their private geometry with kernel functions and leaves the files where they are.
- The descriptors, the element reference, and the host relation of the stage for kinds of element.
- Typed rejections and change sets. They belong to the command layer, and the kernel does not wait for them.
- Performance work beyond building the wall graph once per change. Nothing in this document is a measured time.

Anti-goals. The four in the umbrella specification apply. Four more are specific to this stage.

- A room that loses its name, purpose, ceiling height, or custom outline because a wall near it was edited.
- A wall end that moves by an amount the user can see, without the user moving it. The one exception is the migration in section 5.1, which is bounded and stated.
- A second place that defines a geometric tolerance, added after the first one exists.
- An arc that the plan shows and the 3D view does not, on `main`, at any commit.

## 5. The work

"The kernel" in this document means two folders, `core/geometry/` and `core/topology/`. Names in code font that do not exist on `main` are working names. The builder may change them.

### 5.1 The tolerance module

Today:

- The wall graph merges end points within 1 mm (`core/topology/wall-graph.ts:26`). The merge takes the first point it saw and is not transitive (`core/topology/wall-graph.ts:44-50`).
- "Parallel" is a cross product below 1e-9 in three files (`core/geometry/segment.ts:4`, `core/geometry/polygon.ts:4`, `core/scene/opening-reach.ts:15`). The cross product is not normalized (`core/geometry/segment.ts:21-23`), so the same angle passes for short walls and fails for long ones.
- "The same point" is 1 mm in the graph, 1e-6 in the junction fills (`core/topology/junction-fill.ts:50`), 1e-9 in the opening voids (`core/scene/opening-void.ts:39`), and exact equality in the wall tool (`editor/plan/wall-tool.ts:30-31`).
- A face counts as a room above 1 mm² (`core/topology/rooms.ts:50`), and a fill has area above 1 mm² (`core/topology/junction-fill.ts:47`).
- Offset corners are snapped to a grid of 1e-6 mm (`SNAP_UNITS_PER_MM`, `core/geometry/polygon.ts:21`, ADR-0134).
- The wall face geometry treats a stretch shorter than 1e-9 mm as no stretch (`DEGENERATE_LENGTH_MM`, `core/geometry/wall-face.ts:11`). The room walk names its 1 mm² limit `MIN_ROOM_AREA` (`core/topology/rooms.ts:50`).
- The daylight work added `OUTLINE_TOLERANCE_MM`, 1 mm, for a daylight outline (`core/daylight/room-validation.ts:16`). It is exported, and the side test in `core/daylight/room-geometry.ts` probes at twice this distance (`SIDE_PROBE_MM`, same file, line 37).
- The work on exterior walls (ADR-0218) added `ROOM_RING_TOLERANCE_MM` (`core/scene/wall-room-sides.ts:25`): how far a room corner may lie from a wall centerline and still count as on it. It is set equal to the graph's 1 mm merge on purpose, so that every corner of a derived room counts as on the walls it was traced from.
- Six more constants compare computed geometry with their own number: `DEGENERATE_SEGMENT_EPSILON` (`core/geometry/segment.ts:7`), `DEGENERATE_EDGE_EPSILON` (`core/geometry/polygon.ts:7`), `CONTACT_EPSILON_MM` (`core/scene/walk-collision.ts:46`), `DIRECTION_TIE_TOLERANCE` (`core/scene/front-direction.ts:29`, owned by the work on the view from the front, #627), `BOUNDARY_EPSILON` (`engine/scene/wall-builder.ts:313`), and `CAP_AREA_EPSILON` (`engine/scene/wall-prism.ts:77`).
- A few guards outside plan geometry carry names of the same kind: `PITCH_LIMIT_EPSILON_RAD` (`core/scene/walk-camera.ts:11`), `ZENITH_ALIGNMENT_COSINE_EPSILON` (`core/environment/solar-position.ts:57`), and the two color tolerances in `core/color/color-accuracy.ts` (lines 35 and 111).

Design:

1. One module in `core/geometry/` defines the tolerances and the predicates that use them: same point, zero length, point on a path, parallel, same line or same circle, and zero area. No other file compares computed geometry with its own number.

2. The length tolerance is 0.001 mm. Two points closer than that are the same point. The derivation has two sides.
   - Below the display. The smallest display step is 1/256 inch, which is 0.0992 mm. The tolerance is 99 times smaller. Two values one display step apart can never merge, and a merge can never move a point by an amount the formatter would show.
   - Above the rounding. A 64-bit number 1 km from the origin has a spacing of about 1.2e-10 mm. The tolerance is about 8.6 million times that. A chain of 10,000 transforms that each add one rounding error drifts by about 1.2e-6 mm in the worst case, which is still about 860 times below the tolerance. These figures follow from the number format. They are not measurements.

   The value sits nearer the display side on purpose. Data from other tools often carries three to six decimals of a millimetre, and a tolerance of 0.001 mm joins points that such rounding pulled apart.

3. Two segments are parallel when the shorter one, laid along the direction of the other, ends within the length tolerance of it. This is a length test, so it means the same for a short wall and a long one. For a direction with no length, the angle tolerance is 1e-8 radians: the angle at which a line 100 m long drifts sideways by one length tolerance.

4. Two walls lie on the same line, or the same circle, when every point of the shorter one is within the length tolerance of the other's line or circle. A pair that passes this test is an overlap (section 5.5) and is never intersected. The crossing point of two nearly parallel lines is badly conditioned along the lines, so the kernel must not build a junction from it.

5. A face is degenerate when its area is at most the length tolerance times its perimeter, which means its mean width is below the tolerance. This replaces the two fixed areas of 1 mm².

6. Derived coordinates that must meet exactly, such as offset corners, are rounded to a grid of 1e-6 mm. This is the value ADR-0134 already uses. It is now stated as one thousandth of the length tolerance. Stored coordinates are never rounded.

7. The margins above hold for coordinates within 1 km of the plan origin. Beyond that they shrink in proportion. The property tests generate input up to that extent. Nothing refuses a larger plan in this stage.

8. Not every distance is a tolerance. A tolerance decides whether two computed quantities are equal. The following are distances a person or a rule works with, and they stay in their files. The architecture test of this section carries them on a list, each with its reason.

   Two more distances are rules, not tolerances, and live in the module as named entries rather than on this list: the join distance of point 9 (1 mm, an input rule) and the daylight outline distance of point 12 (1 mm, an acceptance rule for daylight input).

   The six constants of the "Today" list that compare computed geometry are tolerances, and so are the graph's 1 mm merge, `ROOM_RING_TOLERANCE_MM`, `MIN_ROOM_AREA`, the fill's area limit, `SNAP_UNITS_PER_MM`, and `DEGENERATE_LENGTH_MM` of the wall face geometry. Pull request 2 moves each into the module with its current value. 9. The 1 mm merge moves from the derivation to the input. When the pointer places or moves a wall end within the join distance of a junction or of a wall, the editor writes the end exactly onto that junction or wall before it builds the command. The join distance is 1 mm, the value the graph uses today. A typed value is never moved (ADR-0181, point 3). Snapping already writes exact coordinates, so this rule matters only when snapping is off or misses.

10. Existing projects need a migration, because a room whose corners are 0.5 mm apart is closed under the old rule and open under the new one. The migration carries its own copy of the old graph rule, so a later change to the kernel cannot change what it does. The copy freezes `core/topology/wall-graph.ts` and the two segment helpers it calls (`segmentIntersection` and `pointOnSegment` of `core/geometry/segment.ts`) as of `8d922cf2`, because pull request 13 changes those helpers. Its two numbers are frozen named entries of the tolerance module, each with the reason "migration to version 18, never changes": the 1 mm merge and the 1e-9 parallel limit. A version step is right here even though no field changes, because the meaning of "the same point" in stored data changes and a migration needs a version (ADR-0178). It runs that rule once on each floor and writes the result into the walls, in this order. Each bound is marked as derived from the old rule or as expected and not measured.
    1. Walls of 1 mm or less. The old graph skips a wall whose ends are within 1 mm (`core/topology/wall-graph.ts:53`), so such a wall has no junction and bounds no room today. Under the new rule a wall between 0.001 mm and 1 mm long would become a real edge and could make new tees and tiny faces. The migration sets the end of such a wall to its start, which moves the end by at most 1 mm (derived). The wall keeps its id, its openings, and its paint. The derivation then sets it aside as a wall of no length (section 5.5, point 5), so it stays as invisible as it is today. Nothing is deleted.
    2. Junctions. Each other wall end takes the point of the vertex that the old merge gave it, which is the first vertex within 1 mm (`core/topology/wall-graph.ts:44-50`). An end moves by at most 1 mm in this step (derived). All ends at one vertex take the same point.
    3. Tees. The old graph splits a wall at every vertex within 1 mm of the segment between its merged ends (`core/topology/wall-graph.ts:110-117`). A vertex that splits a wall and is not an end of that wall is a tee on it. The migration moves the vertex, with every wall end at it, to the nearest point on the met wall's current line. In this step an end moves by at most 1 mm plus the distance that the met wall's line has moved at that point (derived).
    4. A vertex that is a tee on two or more walls goes to the crossing point of the lines of the first two of them, in the order of the floor's wall list, if that point is within 2 mm of the vertex. A point within 1 mm of two lines is within 2 mm of their crossing when the lines meet at 60 degrees or more (derived). Otherwise the vertex goes onto the line of the first wall, and the report of step 8 counts it.
    5. Repetition. Moving a tee vertex can move the line of a wall that another tee meets. Steps 3 and 4 repeat over every tee until a pass moves no vertex by more than 1e-6 mm, the rounding grid of point 6. A chain of k tees, each on a wall whose ends are tees of the next, settles in at most k + 1 passes (derived). The migration stops after 8 passes. It does not refuse the document: it keeps the result of the eighth pass, and the report names the floor. Chains deeper than 7 tees, and cycles of tees that do not settle in 8 passes, are expected not to occur (not measured).
    6. Near crossings. Under the old rule, two crossings of wall middles within 1 mm of each other share one vertex, because crossings go through the same merge (`core/topology/wall-graph.ts:93-94`). Under the new rule they are separate points, and three walls that cross this way leave a face less than about 1 mm across. That face is above the degenerate size of point 5, so the derivation keeps it as a room with no record. The migration moves only wall ends and cannot repair this, so it leaves such a cluster as it is and counts it. A user removes the small room by moving one of the walls. A cluster needs three walls that pass through nearly one point away from their ends, so it is expected to be rare (not measured).
    7. Openings and dimensions. An opening keeps the plan point of its center: its `position` becomes the distance from the wall's new start to that point, projected onto the new wall line. The center moves sideways by at most the larger move of the wall's two ends (derived). If the opening would then reach past an end of the wall, its position is clamped so that it ends at that end, which moves it along the wall by at most the move of that end (derived), and the report counts it. A dimension point that sat exactly on a wall end moves with that end. Any other dimension point stays, so a dimension measured to the middle of a moved wall can change by at most the move of that wall's ends (derived).
    8. The report. The migration returns its counts: ends moved and the largest move, walls set to no length, tees that took the second rule of step 4, near-crossing clusters, floors that reached the pass limit, and clamped openings. The report also counts the walls whose formatted length changes at the default precision. The pull request runs it over every format fixture and every version fixture and puts the counts in its description. It compares rooms before and after under the overlap rule of section 5.5, which pull request 12 already landed, so that a fixture with an overlap is not counted as changed. The migration adds no dialog or notice, and the migration backup of ADR-0182 keeps the original. Two things can change on screen. A tee vertex moved onto the met wall's line gets coordinates off the grid, so a wall that ends there can lose its exact length label and gain the approximate mark of section 5.8, or the reverse. And a near-crossing cluster of step 6 leaves a room less than 1 mm across in the plan and the 3D view.

    So an end at a corner moves at most 1 mm, an end at a tee on a wall whose own ends do not move by step 3 moves at most 2 mm, and each further level of a chain of tees adds at most 1 mm (derived). Most ends are expected not to move at all, because snapping wrote them exactly (not measured). The migration takes the next free `schemaVersion` when its pull request lands (18 today: `CURRENT_SCHEMA_VERSION` is 17 at `core/model/factories.ts:43`). A version step starts the revision count of the new version at 0 (ADR-0178), so `CURRENT_SCHEMA_REVISION` goes back to 0 in the same pull request.

    The alternative is to keep the 1 mm merge for old documents. That leaves two meanings of "the same point" for as long as old documents exist, and it keeps the defect ADR-0181 names: a merge that can move a wall end by about 1/25 inch.

11. The module lands in two steps. First every geometric constant moves into it with its current value, and the 1 mm merge keeps working. Then the pull request with the migration switches the derivation to the length tolerance. Between the two, behavior does not change. The length tolerance has a use from the first step: the rounding grid of point 6 is defined from it.

12. The daylight outline distance. Pull request 2 moves `OUTLINE_TOLERANCE_MM` from `core/daylight/room-validation.ts:16` into the module, with its value of 1 mm, as a named distance of its own. `room-validation.ts` and `room-geometry.ts` import it from there, and the daylight lane reviews that pull request. Today:

- A wall is two end points (`core/model/types.ts:76-90`), and a wall scene node repeats them (`core/scene/scene-graph.ts:42-62`).

- A search for a variable with "wall" in its name followed by `.start` or `.end` finds 82 lines with 129 reads in 25 production files: 11 files of `core/`, 13 of `editor/`, and 1 of `engine/` (`engine/scene/wall-scene-helpers.ts:14-15`). The full number was not measured.

Design:

1. A wall path is a value that describes a wall's centerline: a straight line between two points, or a circular arc between two points. The kernel builds it from a wall with one function, and every other file works with the path through kernel functions.
2. The functions cover what the wall-reading files do today with end points: the two ends, the length, the point and the tangent and the normal at a distance along the path, the nearest point on the path to a given point with its distance along and its offset, the bounds, a part of the path between two distances, the path offset to one side, the path under a transform, and the path flattened to a stated deviation.
3. A position along a wall is a distance along its path. An opening's `position` already means that for a straight wall (`core/model/types.ts:107-108`), so no stored value changes.
4. The wall scene node carries the path and no end points. A file that still wants `wall.start` on a scene node then fails to compile, which is the strongest form of the rule.
5. The model type keeps `start` and `end`, because they are the file format. For the model type the rule is held by a test that uses the type checker: outside the kernel folders, no expression reads `start` or `end` from a value of the wall type. It also flags a value of the wall type passed to a parameter whose declared type has `start` or `end`, such as `distanceAlongWall(wall: { start: Point; end: Point })` (`core/scene/wall-room-sides.ts:145`), so a structural parameter cannot hide a read. Migrations and the schema work on untyped data and are outside the rule. Commands that change a wall's ends do it through kernel functions.
6. The move happens in the open. The accessor lands with an exception list that names every file the check of point 5 finds, at least 26. It lands with one real use: the same pull request moves `engine/scene/wall-scene-helpers.ts` to the path, so the seam has a reader from its first day. Each later pull request takes files off the list. The list is empty before any arc can reach a user.
7. The accessor does not depend on the trial of section 5.3. A path describes one wall's centerline, and the inner form of the plan geometry does not change it. So the accessor and its moves run beside the trial.

Left to the builder: the names and signatures, and the cut of the wall-reading files into pull requests. Section 6 proposes one by layer.

- The exception list is empty and the test of point 5 passes with no entry.
- The wall scene node has no `start` and no `end`.

- Every baseline is unchanged by these pull requests, or the pull request says why one moved.

### 5.5 Overlapping walls and other degenerate input

Today:

- The graph handles crossings and tees. Two segments on one line give no intersection (`core/geometry/segment.ts:22`), so a shared stretch becomes two edges between the same two junctions. ADR-0026 lists this case as out of scope.
- The review ran four cases against the room derivation: a square, a wall inside one edge, a wall that overlaps one edge and runs past the corner, and an exact copy of one edge. The last three give no rooms (#681). This document did not run them again. From the code, the cause is the face walk: two edges leave a junction at the same angle, the sort has no rule for that, and the walk joins the room to the outer face.
- A wall of no length is skipped (`core/topology/wall-graph.ts:53`).
- The add-wall handler appends the wall with no check (`core/commands/handlers/wall-commands.ts:44-50`).

Design, the derivation:

1. The derivation is total. For any list of walls it returns a plan geometry, and no input makes a room disappear that the walls enclose.
2. Two walls overlap when they lie on the same line or the same circle (section 5.1, point 4) and share a stretch longer than the length tolerance. The graph makes one edge for the shared stretch.
3. The wall that comes first in the floor's wall list owns the shared edge. The footprint, the 3D solid, the faces, and the room boundary of that stretch come from the owner. The other wall is recorded on the edge as covered and contributes only the parts of itself that no earlier wall covers. Nothing is drawn twice.
4. A wall that is covered over its whole length contributes nothing. The plan geometry lists it, so that a later stage can show it to the user.
5. A wall shorter than the length tolerance is set aside and listed, as today.
6. Two edges that leave a junction in the same direction can still exist once arcs exist, for example a straight wall and an arc that starts tangent to it. The fan is ordered by direction first and by curvature second, so the order is always defined.

Design, the command (decided on 17 November 2026: a wall drawn over a wall merges):

7. A wall drawn over part of another wall merges with it. The add-wall command keeps the parts of the drawn wall that no existing wall covers and adds each as a wall. The existing wall is not changed: it keeps its id, its thickness, its construction, its openings, and its paint. If nothing is left, no wall is added. In both cases the plan says so in one line, for example "Part of this wall was already there." The whole act is one undo step. Where the line shows is left to the builder: if the command layer has built a place for notes on commands by then, the line goes there.
8. "Merge" here does not join two walls into one longer wall. Joining would give the result a new extent, and every opening on it is measured from the wall's start.
9. One function in the kernel computes the parts. The handler calls it, and the wall tool calls it for the preview, so the user sees what will be added before the click.
10. The cost: the document holds less than the user drew. If the user meant to give the overdrawn stretch another thickness, that intent is dropped and only the line in the plan says so.

The option that was not chosen, "refuse": points 7 to 10 become a rule that rejects the wall. The editor checks the rule first and shows the reason, and a chained run of walls ends at the refused segment. The handler throws on a direct dispatch until the command layer brings typed rejections. Points 1 to 6 stay exactly as they are, because old documents, a move, and a paste can still hold an overlap. The cost of refusing: tracing over an underlay stops in the middle of a run, and the user has to find where the existing wall ends and start again from there.

A third option is to keep both walls in the document and let the derivation show one. Points 1 to 6 already do that for every overlap that does not come from drawing. As the rule for drawing it has a cost that grows: covered walls pile up where nobody can see or select them, and one reappears when its owner is moved or deleted.

Other commands can leave an overlap: an end point drag, a move, a paste. This stage does not change them. The derivation gives a defined result, and the command layer decides whether they merge or refuse when it moves rules into the handlers (ADR-0179).

Left to the builder: the wording of the line in the plan, and how the preview shows the parts.

Done when:

- The four cases of #681 are tests, and each yields one room with the area of the square.
- A property test adds a wall along any stretch of an existing wall and finds the same rooms with the same areas.
- Drawing over part of a wall adds only the uncovered part, changes no existing wall, and is one undo step.
- Drawing a wall fully inside another adds nothing and shows the line.
- A document that already holds two overlapping walls opens, shows each stretch once in the plan and in 3D, and lists the covered wall.

### 5.6 Room records: how a room keeps its identity

Today:

- A room's id is the sorted ids of its bounding walls joined with `|` (`core/topology/rooms.ts:72`, `core/topology/rooms.ts:172`). The user's data for a room is stored under that key in `Project.roomOverrides` (`core/model/types.ts:309`) and found by exact match (`core/topology/rooms.ts:88`).
- The key is taken from every half-edge of the face, before dead-end stubs are removed (`core/topology/rooms.ts:424`, `core/topology/rooms.ts:437`). So a short stub wall drawn inside a room adds its id to the key. The room gets a new id, and its name, purpose, period, style, ceiling height, and custom outline no longer match. Nothing is deleted, and nothing tells the user.
- Deleting a wall and drawing it again gives a new wall id and the same result.
- Paint does not have this fault. A wall face is keyed by wall id and side, and a floor or ceiling by floor id (`core/model/paint.ts:51-62`).
- The inspector takes the key from the room's id (`editor/shell/inspector.tsx:275`).
- Once arcs exist the key has a second fault. A round room of two arc walls, divided by a partition that crosses both arcs, gives two rooms with the same three walls and so the same id.

Design:

1. The user's data for a room is a room record, stored in the document as a list. A record holds:
   - an id of its own, which never changes;
   - the floor it is on;
   - its boundary: the wall faces that bounded its room when the record was last bound, each a wall id and a side;
   - its anchor: a point inside the room at that time;
   - the fields the override holds today.
2. Rooms are still derived from the walls and never stored (ADR-0026). A record is not a room. It is user data that looks for its room.
3. The kernel binds records to rooms with one rule, and the binding is a pure function of the document:
   - The score of a record for a room is the share of the record's boundary faces that bound that room.
   - A pair is admitted when its score is one, or when its score is at least one half and the room holds the record's anchor. A record with no anchor yet (a migrated record before its floor's first edit, point 10) never reaches this rule, because point 4 writes its anchor first.
   - Admitted pairs are taken in order: highest score first, then the room that holds the record's anchor, then the larger room, then the earlier record. Each record gets at most one room and each room at most one record.
   - A record still without a room takes the room that holds its anchor, if that room has no record.
   - A record that is still left over is unbound. It is kept and listed.

   Open: the owner decides the anchor condition for a score below one. It is written here as the working assumption, recommended. Without it, a common edit names the wrong room: in a row of rooms under one long outside wall, deleting the end wall of a named room leaves its neighbor bounded by half of the record's faces (the long wall's inner face and the floor's other long wall), and an unnamed neighbor takes the name for good. The other options are to score by the length of the shared face stretches, which needs a new stored field, or to keep the rule without the condition and accept that case.

4. After every command that changes a floor's wall list, one step rewrites each bound record of that floor: its boundary becomes the room's current faces, and its anchor moves only if it is no longer inside the room. Unbound records are not touched. Before the handler runs, the same wrapper gives every record of that floor that has no anchor yet its sides and its anchor, bound against the rooms of the floor as it was: there the record still matches its room, so the edit that follows binds it with the full rule. The binding and the rewrite are kernel functions in `core/topology/`. They read the wall network through the one memo of section 5.3, point 9, so the step builds no second network. The wrapper is one function in `core/commands/`, registered once beside the project commands, and it runs inside the command, so undo reverts it with the rest. No handler calls it by name: the registration wraps every project command, runs the handler, and then runs the step for each floor whose wall list the handler replaced. The command layer specification takes this step as an input and runs it after a handler's apply and before the change set is computed. When the command layer brings change sets, the step becomes a reader of the change set.
5. What this gives the user:

   | Edit                                               | Result                                                                                                                                                                         |
   | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
   | A stub or a tee wall is added                      | Nothing changes. Every face of the record still bounds the room.                                                                                                               |
   | A wall is deleted and drawn again, or is split     | Nothing changes. The other faces carry the record, and the step then learns the new wall.                                                                                      |
   | A wall is dragged and the room shrinks or grows    | Nothing changes. Faces do not depend on position.                                                                                                                              |
   | The whole plan is moved, turned, or mirrored       | Nothing changes, for the same reason.                                                                                                                                          |
   | A partition divides the room                       | The part that keeps the larger share of the record's wall faces keeps the record. On a tie, the part that holds the anchor. The other part is a room with no record.           |
   | A partition between two named rooms is removed     | The merged room keeps one record. The other is unbound and kept.                                                                                                               |
   | That partition is drawn again                      | Both rooms have their records again.                                                                                                                                           |
   | Every wall of a room is deleted and new ones drawn | The record is unbound while the room is gone, and its anchor finds the new room.                                                                                               |
   | An outside wall of a named room is deleted         | The room is gone and the record is unbound. No neighbor takes it, because no neighbor holds its anchor. When a new outside wall closes the room again, the record binds to it. |

6. A room with a record has an id made from the record's id. A room without one has an id made from its faces. That id is different from every record id, and it is different for the two halves of the round room above, because faces carry a side. The first command that sets a field on a room creates its record, with an id fixed when the command is made, so redo gives the same record.
7. A record whose last field is cleared is removed. Records exist only while they hold something the user entered.
8. The room commands take the record id, or the room's current id when the room has no record yet. The lookups in `core/architecture-era/` do the same.
9. An unbound record is never deleted by the application. The cost of that: a name can come back. A user who deletes a named room and later draws a new room around the same spot finds the old name on it. This document accepts that, because the other choice is to delete user data when walls are deleted. A list of unbound records with a control to delete them is later work (section 9).
10. The migration. `roomOverrides` is a map keyed by wall ids. The step to the next free `schemaVersion` when its pull request lands (19, if the step of section 5.1 took 18 and no other step landed between them) rewrites it as the list of records:
    - a record's id is its old key, which is unique in the map and needs no random value;
    - its boundary is the wall ids of the key, with no side, because finding the side needs a derivation and a migration must not depend on the live kernel. A face with no side matches either side. The first wall command on that floor writes the sides and the anchor before its handler runs (point 4), so the first edit after an upgrade binds the record as later edits do;
    - its floor is the floor that holds its walls;
    - a key whose walls no longer exist becomes a record with no floor. It was already dead under the old rule, and it is kept, as the migration to version 17 kept such keys;
    - every field of every override is in the output. The test shows it.

    The existing migration to version 17 imports `roomKey` from the kernel (`core/migrations/schema/rekey-room-overrides.ts:1`). It gets its own copy of that one line, so removing `roomKey` cannot change an old migration. The format test `tests/format/room-overrides-binding.test.ts` moves to the records in the same pull request.

Why not a simpler rule. Two were weighed. A point alone, where the record belongs to whichever room holds it, breaks when a wall is dragged past the point: the neighbor takes the name. Faces alone, with no point, lose a room that is rebuilt with new walls. Each of the two covers the other's gap. Keeping the key of today and repairing it after each edit was not weighed further, because the key cannot tell the two halves of a divided round room apart.

Left to the builder: the names, the shape of a record, how the anchor is chosen (it must be inside the clear boundary and the same for the same input), and how the editor keeps a room selected when its first record is created.

Done when:

- Each row of the table in point 5 is a test. The row for a divided room has two cases: a partition that cuts off a corner closet while the anchor lies in the closet, where the main part keeps the record by the share of faces, and a partition across the full room, where the two parts tie and the part with the anchor keeps it.
- A record migrated from version 17, whose floor's first edit after the upgrade deletes and redraws every wall of its room, or deletes the room's outside wall, gives the result of the matching row of point 5.
- The step and the scene graph together build the changed floor's wall network once per wall command (section 5.3, point 9).
- A property test splits any wall of any generated plan in two and finds every record on the room that holds its anchor.
- A property test shows that binding does not depend on the order of the wall list, apart from the last tie-break.
- The two halves of a divided round room have different ids.
- A document at version 17 with room overrides has, after the migration and one edit, the same name, purpose, period, style, ceiling height, and custom outline on every room as before.
- Naming a room keeps it selected.
- No path deletes a record that holds a field.

### 5.7 The arc segment

Today:

- A wall is a straight segment. The format reserves the name `wall.curve` for curved walls (`docs/specs/2026-06-10-vernacular-floor-plan-format.md`, section 6.5) and says a reserved name becomes a defined field only by an increment of `schemaRevision` or `schemaVersion`, with a decision record. Section 7 of that document counts the promotion of a reserved name to an optional field as additive.
- `core/` already has an exact arc for opening heads: a segment of a contour with an end, a center, and a direction (`core/scene/contour.ts:4-6`, ADR-0125). The engine owns its tessellation.
- The document codec on `main` reads and writes `meta.schemaRevision`. `CURRENT_SCHEMA_REVISION` is 1 (`core/model/factories.ts:49`): revision 1 of version 17 added the field itself, and `schema/17/revision-1/` holds its schema. A file at a higher revision than the build's is read tolerantly and reported as saved by a newer build (`core/format/codec/revision.ts`, `checkModeFor`).

As stored:

1. A wall gains one optional field, `curve`. Absent means straight. The one defined form is an arc, and it holds one number, the bulge: the rise of the arc at the middle of the wall, divided by half the straight distance between the wall's ends. A positive bulge curves to the left when looking from the wall's start to its end, which is the side the opening orientation already calls positive (`core/model/types.ts:94-99`). The field has a `kind`, so later curve forms can be added beside the arc.
2. The bulge is at most 1 in size, which is a half circle. A round room is two arc walls. A tower that turns three quarters of a circle is two arc walls. Larger arcs are a later, additive change.

   The centerline radius has a lower bound against the wall's resolved thickness, because the inner face of a thick wall on a tight arc does not exist below half the thickness. Open: the owner decides the bound. The working assumption, recommended: the centerline radius is at least the wall's resolved thickness. The other options are a bound just above half the thickness, where the inner face can come to a point, or no bound in this stage. Under the working assumption the bend handle stops at the bound, the Radius field refuses a radius below it with a reason, and the thickness command refuses a thickness that would break it on an arc wall. The derivation stays total for a stored arc that breaks the bound (from an older build or a hand-edited file): it draws that wall straight and lists it with what was set aside.

3. Why the bulge and not a radius or a center:
   - It cannot disagree with the end points. A stored center can lie at different distances from the two ends, and a stored radius can be shorter than half the wall.
   - Every value in the allowed range is a valid arc. No edit to an end point can make it invalid.
   - `start` and `end` keep their meaning, so junctions, the migration of section 5.1, and every tool that moves an end work as they do for a straight wall.
   - Moving an end keeps the shape. A half-round bay stays half round when it is widened. The radius follows.
   - Moving, turning, and scaling a wall leave the number alone. A mirror flips its sign.
4. A radius is still what a person reads and types. The radius is computed from the bulge and the chord, and a typed radius is converted to a bulge. The conversion is exact to far below the length tolerance, so a typed radius reads back as typed (section 5.8).

The file format change:

5. `wall.curve` is a new optional field and the promotion of a reserved name. Under ADR-0178 and section 7 of the format specification that is additive: it takes the next `schemaRevision` of the version that is current when its pull request lands, and no migration. With both version steps of this stage before it, that is revision 1 of version 19 if no other revision of 19 lands first. There is no fallback to a version step. Section 6.5 of the format specification changes the status of `wall.curve` from reserved to defined. The design of the view from the front (#627) uses the same rule for its two optional fields. Its revisions are planned as revisions of the version on `main` when each lands, 17 today. A version step resets the revision to 0 (ADR-0178), so after a version step of this stage lands, a field not yet published becomes a revision of the new version. Fields already published at that moment are part of the new version's schema. Each lane takes the next free number when it lands and checks it again before the merge.
6. What an older build does with such a file follows from the reading rules. A build at an older version refuses the file. A build at the same version and an older revision opens it, keeps the field, says a newer build saved it, and draws the wall straight. The second case can only be a build from between two kernel pull requests, since both version steps of this stage land before the arc.
7. Two alternatives were weighed and are not taken:
   - Define `wall.curve` in the schema of this stage's own room-records version step (pull request 14). It needs no `schemaRevision` and no third version step, and an older build at that version draws the arc straight, as in the revision case. Its cost is a schema field with no writer until pull request 18, and a published schema is fixed once released (ADR-0178), so the field would be fixed before the arc that writes it is built.
   - A third version step with a migration that changes nothing. Every older build would then refuse the file, and it is the kind of step ADR-0178 was written to stop.

   The cost of the rule taken: a build at the same version and a lower revision draws an arc wall straight (point 6). The document codec is on `main`, so the rule no longer holds the arc back.

In the kernel:

8. The path of an arc wall is an exact arc. The graph finds where a line meets an arc and where two arcs meet, splits an arc at a tee, and treats two arcs on one circle as an overlap (section 5.5).
9. A footprint of an arc edge is bounded by two arcs around the same center, one per face. A miter corner is the exact meeting point of the two face curves, line with arc or arc with arc. Using the tangent line in place of the arc would put the corner off by several millimetres for a thick wall on a tight radius. Two cases need a rule. When an arc leaves a junction tangent to a line, within the angle tolerance, the two face curves touch at one point and their meeting point is badly conditioned, so the corner is the offset point of the junction on each face. When a face line meets a face circle twice, the corner is the meeting point nearer the junction along both faces.
10. A room boundary can hold arcs. The clear boundary of an arc edge is an arc around the same center. The area is exact: the area of the straight-sided outline plus or minus the circular segments. A consumer that needs straight edges asks for the boundary flattened to a deviation it names. The area is never computed from a flattened outline.

As drawn:

11. In the plan, the wall's fill, face lines, selection, and paint bands follow the arc, and the canvas draws true arcs. Hit testing and snapping use the nearest point on the arc. The snap points of an arc wall are its two ends and the nearest point. The SVG export writes an arc.
12. In 3D, an arc edge is one solid with two curved faces, built from the footprint of point 9 and tessellated in `engine/`, which already owns tessellation for curved opening heads. Each face stays one paintable face, so paint and the 3D face pick work as for a straight wall. Walk collision uses the flattened path.

Making one (decided on 17 November 2026: bend a straight wall):

13. A straight wall is bent. A selected wall shows a handle at its middle, beside the two end handles it has today. Dragging the handle sideways sets the bulge, and the plan, the 3D view, and the inspector follow. The handle stops at a half circle and snaps back to straight near the chord. The wall keeps its id, its junctions, its paint, and its room.
14. The wall inspector gains a Radius field next to the thickness field, built from the length field that exists. It shows the radius of an arc wall and is empty for a straight one. A typed radius shorter than half the wall is refused with a reason. Clearing the field makes the wall straight.
15. One command sets or clears a wall's curve. A drag is one undo step.
16. Why bending: the same wall changes from straight to arc and back, with its id and everything attached to it. That is the hardest case for the accessor and the caches, so it is the best proof of them. It needs no new tool, so it adds nothing to the lists of tool ids that the stage for tools and input will replace. For tracing an underlay it takes the same three inputs as a three-point tool: two ends and a point on the curve.
17. The cost: a curved wall takes two acts, draw and then bend, and nothing in the tool rack says that walls can curve. A person has to find the handle.

The option that was not chosen, the three-point arc tool: points 1 to 12 do not change. Points 13 to 15 are replaced by a tool that takes a start, an end, and a point on the curve. A new tool adds an id to about six shared lists that the stage for tools and input replaces: the `ToolId` union (`editor/tools/tool-types.ts:1-8`), the `toolLabel` switch (`editor/tools/tool-label.ts:4-18`), the authoring list and switch (`editor/plan/use-plan-authoring.ts:54-59`, `150-170`), `PLACEMENT_TOOLS` (`editor/plan/use-escape-to-select.ts:8`), `isCreativeAuthoringTool` (`editor/plan/plan-overlay.tsx:75`), the tools panel, and a shortcut. That work is done twice. An arc mode inside the wall tool would add no id. The last pull request grows, and bending an existing wall is no longer possible unless both are built.

The other option that was not chosen, a typed radius alone: point 14 stays and point 13 goes. The last pull request shrinks. The cost moves to the user: a radius says nothing about which side the wall curves to, so the field needs a second control, and tracing a curve from an underlay becomes trial and error.

Limits of this stage:

18. An arc wall hosts no opening, and a wall that hosts an opening cannot be bent. A file can still hold one, written by an older build that drew the arc straight or edited by hand, and the schema cannot forbid it. The derivation stays total: the opening gets its span along the arc in the plan, no void is cut in 3D, and the opening is listed with what was set aside. The bend handle and the Radius field do not show on such a wall, the opening tool does not offer an arc wall, and the handlers refuse both on a direct dispatch. A door in a curved wall needs a decision about the shape of its void and its leaf in 3D, and that belongs to the design round for the rest of #80. The kernel functions already take a position along an arc, so that round starts from a working accessor.
19. The arc reaches users in the last arc pull request, which adds the stored field, the command, the handle, and the field in the inspector. The three pull requests before it teach the kernel, the plan, and the 3D view to handle an arc path, and only tests and stories build one. This is a deliberate, short exception to the rule that a seam lands with a use. The alternative is one pull request too large to review.
20. The arc comes after the trial of section 5.3 and after the consumer moves. That is the cost of decision 5: the arc reaches users about one trial and one approval later than it would without the trial. The arc work itself does not depend on the outcome of the trial, because the fan order and the noding are the same in both designs.

Left to the builder: the names, the look of the handle, the snap distance to straight, the tessellation density in 3D, and the wording of the refusals.

Done when:

- A wall with a curve is saved, loaded, and equal. A file with the field passes the schema of the new revision.
- An arc with a bulge near zero gives the same rooms, footprints, and areas as the straight wall, within the tolerance.
- Swapping a wall's ends and flipping the sign of its bulge gives the same geometry.
- A round room of two half-circle walls with a radius of 1500 mm on the centerline reports the area of the circle inside its wall faces.
- A journey bends a wall, sees the arc in the plan and in 3D, saves, reloads, and sees it again. A scene baseline and a plan story baseline hold the arc.
- The exported SVG holds an arc for that wall.
- Bending a wall with a door is not offered, and a direct dispatch changes nothing.
- A stored arc wall that hosts an opening, or that breaks the radius bound of point 2a, opens without an error and is listed with what was set aside.
- A line meeting an arc tangent at a junction, and a face line that meets a face circle twice, each give the corner that point 9 states.
- A typed radius reads back as typed.

### 5.8 Lengths that read back as typed

Today:

- Lengths are typed in inspector fields built from `LengthField` (`editor/plan/length-field.tsx`): the opening, furniture, wall thickness, and ceiling height editors. No field sets a wall's length.
- The wall tool takes a point from a click, or from Enter at a keyboard candidate that the arrow keys move in 100 mm steps (`editor/plan/keyboard-candidate.ts:3`, `editor/plan/authoring-tool-handlers.ts:118-129`). It takes no typed length (`editor/plan/wall-tool.ts:62-103`).
- The formatter rounds to the user's precision and says nothing about what it dropped. The default imperial precision is 1/8 inch (`core/units/preferences.ts:18`). In metric the adaptive formatter shows metres at 2 places, centimetres at 1, and millimetres at 0 (`core/units/format-adaptive-length.ts:11-23`). The formatter feeds the plan labels, the dimensions, the drag and draw readouts, the scale bar, the coordinate readout, the tool rail, and the accessible wall label (`editor/plan/label-layout.ts`, `draw-dimension.ts`, `drag-readout.ts`, `draw-readout.ts`, `scale-bar.ts`, `format-coords.ts`, `editor/shell/tool-rail.tsx`, `editor/plan/overlay-label.ts:25`).
- A new project is imperial (`app/create-initial-project.ts:8`). The snap grid is 100 mm in every project (`editor/plan/snap.ts:35`, `editor/plan/use-snapping.ts:59`), and so are the keyboard candidate step and the selection nudge, whose larger step is 1000 mm (`editor/plan/use-selection-keyboard.ts:28-29`). 100 mm is 3.937 inches, which is not a multiple of 1/256 inch.
- While a wall is drawn, the angle snap runs before the grid snap (`editor/plan/snap.ts:354-363`). It projects the cursor onto the nearest 45 degree ray and does not round the length (`editor/plan/snap.ts:213-228`). So a pointer-drawn wall has an arbitrary length in both unit systems, unless its end snaps to an existing point.

Design:

1. The formatter looks for the coarsest step at which the stored value is exact within the length tolerance. It starts at the user's precision and goes down to the smallest display step, 1/256 inch or 0.1 mm. If it finds one, it shows that value in full. If it finds none, it rounds at the user's precision and marks the result as approximate. The user's precision setting is then the coarsest step shown, and no longer a cap.
2. The kernel has one function that places a wall end at a typed length along a direction. It applies the value exactly and does not pass the result through the join rule of section 5.1.
3. The wall inspector gains a Length field beside the thickness field, built from `LengthField`. The Radius field of section 5.7, point 14, joins them in pull request 18. The Length field shows the distance between the wall's ends through the formatter of point 1.
   - Today every field built from `LengthField` shows its value through `formatEntryMagnitude` (`editor/plan/length-field.tsx:125`, `core/units/format-entry-magnitude.ts:15-21`): a bare decimal in the entry unit, at 2 places for inches, 3 for feet and metres, and 0 for millimetres. A thickness typed as 4 9/16 inches then shows as 0.38 in the default unit of feet, and no field shows a fraction or 0.1 mm. So `LengthField` itself moves to the formatter of point 1, for every field built from it, or no field can read back a typed value. After a commit, the field shows the formatted length (for example 4 9/16"), because a typed fraction must read back as it was typed. The owner chose this on 18 November. The alternative was a bare number in the entry unit that accepts fractions. Either choice moves the baselines of every length field story.
   - A typed length keeps the wall's start and moves its end along the direction from start to end, through the function of point 2. The start stays because an opening's `position` is measured from the start (`core/model/types.ts:107-108`), so every opening keeps its place.
   - The field dispatches the existing `moveWallEndpoint` command with that end, so no command is added and the edit is one undo step.
   - Walls joined at the moved end are not changed, as with the end drag today. `moveWallEndpoint` moves one end of one wall (`core/commands/handlers/wall-commands.ts:84-91`), and the drag in `editor/plan/use-wall-editing.ts:88-93` dispatches only that command. So a shorter length opens the corner at that end, and a longer one leaves the end past the joined wall. The plan shows either at once.
   - The field refuses, with a reason, a length that would leave an opening past the new end, and a length that is not positive. The check runs in the field. The handler does not change, which leaves the same gap for a direct dispatch that the end drag has today, until the command layer moves rules into handlers (ADR-0179).
   - On an arc wall the field shows the length along the arc and takes no input in this stage.
4. What the user sees. Lengths show the exact value, finer than the precision setting if needed, down to 1/256 inch or 0.1 mm. Any other value carries the approximate mark. A thickness typed as 4 9/16 inches reads 4 9/16 inches at a precision of 1/8 inch. A metric wall of 3000.4 mm reads 3.0004 m where it reads 3.00 m today. A diagonal wall, a wall that ends on another wall's middle, and the coordinates of a free cursor carry the mark. The plan labels, the readouts, and the accessible wall label all change. Story baselines expected to move: the coordinate readout, the dimension inspector, the overall dimensions, the wall inspector, and every length field story (under `e2e/stories/__screenshots__/`). Through the visible grid of point 5, the app screenshots of a new project (`e2e/tests/visual-regression.spec.ts-snapshots/`) are expected to move too, because a new project is imperial. None of this was rendered.
5. The steps of pointer and keyboard input follow the unit system, so that a length drawn with the pointer is exact. In an imperial project the snap grid, the keyboard candidate step, and the selection nudge are 4 inches (101.6 mm), and the larger nudge is 3 feet (914.4 mm). A metric project keeps 100 mm and 1000 mm. While grid snapping is on, the angle snap rounds the length along its ray to the grid step from the run's last point. With Alt held the angle snap is off, as today, and the end snaps to a grid point instead. The visible grid of an imperial project steps in inches and feet, so the snapped points lie on its lines. Without this point, most lengths drawn with the pointer carry the approximate mark in both unit systems, because of the angle snap, and almost every grid point of an imperial project is off any multiple of 1/256 inch. The owner chose on 18 November to build this point now, in the same pull request as point 1, so the labels and baselines of point 4 move once.
6. The round trip is a property test over every inch fraction down to 1/64 and every metric value down to 0.1 mm, in a range of wall lengths and at many angles: type the length in the Length field, save, load, measure, format, and compare with what was typed. A second property places the end through the function of point 2 directly.

The other choice for point 1 keeps the precision setting as a cap: a value that is not exact at the user's precision is rounded and marked. Its cost is that a typed value finer than the precision does not read back as typed (4 9/16 inches shows as an approximate 4 5/8 at 1/8 inch), so goal 8 fails at the default precision and the umbrella's sixth condition holds only at a precision of 1/64 inch.

Left to the builder: the mark for an approximate value, within the design language, the series of steps of the imperial visible grid, and the range of the test.

Done when:

- The property tests of point 6 pass.
- A value that is not exact at any step shows the mark.
- A wall thickness typed as 4 9/16 inches reads back as 4 9/16 inches at a precision of 1/8 inch.
- A length typed in the Length field moves only the wall's end, keeps every opening's plan position, leaves joined walls unchanged, and is one undo step. A length that would leave an opening past the end is refused with a reason.
- In an imperial project a wall drawn with grid and angle snapping on, along a world axis or at 45 degrees, has a length that is a whole number of 4-inch steps and shows no mark.

### 5.9 Property-based tests

Today the repository has no property-based test, and the library the design specification names for them (section 9.2 of that document) is not installed.

Design:

1. The library is added as a development dependency, at an exact version older than the cooldown. Failing seeds are printed so a failure can be replayed.
2. The generators make plans, not random numbers: walls on a jittered grid, closed loops, tees, crossings, stubs, and, on purpose, the hard cases: copies of a wall, walls along part of a wall, walls of no length, junctions a little inside and a little outside the tolerance, pairs that are almost parallel, and, once they exist, arcs.
3. The properties:

   | Group      | Property                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
   | ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
   | Totality   | For any generated plan the derivation returns, and no number in the result is infinite or undefined.                                                                                                                               |
   | Graph      | Every walk around a face closes, and Euler's formula holds. With explicit half-edges (ADR-0200), every half-edge also has a twin and lies on exactly one face. With the fallback, every edge bounds at most one face on each side. |
   | Invariance | Reordering the walls, moving the plan, or turning it gives the same rooms with the same areas, within the tolerance.                                                                                                               |
   | Splits     | Cutting a wall in two at any point changes no room and no area.                                                                                                                                                                    |
   | Overlap    | Adding a wall along an existing wall changes no room and no area.                                                                                                                                                                  |
   | Tolerance  | Two points closer than the tolerance are one junction. Two points farther apart than twice the tolerance are two.                                                                                                                  |
   | Corners    | Two edges that meet at a junction share their miter corner as the same coordinates.                                                                                                                                                |
   | Arcs       | The properties of section 5.7: near-zero bulge, swapped ends, and exact area against a finely flattened outline.                                                                                                                   |
   | Identity   | The properties of section 5.6.                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
   | Lengths    | The round trip of section 5.8.                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
   | Migrations | Each migration of this stage keeps the rooms and the user data of a generated version 17 document.                                                                                                                                 |

4. A property lands with the code that makes it true. Pull request 3 brings only the properties that hold on `main` today. The trial may use its generators on its own branch.
5. The tests run in the fast tier with the unit tests. Their run time was not measured.

Left to the builder: the number of runs per property, and the split of generators into files.

Done when every property in the table is a test and each ran red before its code turned it green, where the code is new.

## 10. Risks

- The stage is large: a trial and 17 code pull requests, two schema versions, and one revision. The plan's estimate was not measured, and many of its cycles hold more than one behavior.
- The trial can overrun its time box or end without a clear answer. Its time box and its stop rule (section 5.3, the trial, point 7) bound that, and the fallback design gives the same result to every consumer, so no consumer move waits on a second trial.
- The arc comes about one trial and one approval later than it would without the trial. Every pull request from 9 on waits for the trial and for the owner's approval of ADR-0200. The work that does not depend on the answer runs beside the trial (decided on 19 November, section 6), so the delay is about the trial's length plus the wait for that approval.
- A trial that passes on fixtures and generated plans can still miss a case. Pull request 9 builds the chosen design again, test first, with a parity test against a frozen copy of today's walk that stays until pull request 12 changes the rooms on purpose.
- The trial may show that half-edges reach no more than the fallback in this stage (section 5.3, the trial, point 5). Then the choice rests on code size and on gains for later work, which the owner weighs.
- The migration of section 5.1 moves stored coordinates. The bound is 1 mm at a corner and 2 mm at a simple tee, and each further level of a chain of tees adds 1 mm (derived). The backup keeps the original, but it is the first migration in this project that changes geometry. Its test compares rooms before and after for every format fixture, and its pull request reports its counts (section 5.1, point 10, step 8). Near-crossing clusters are left as they are and leave a room less than 1 mm across in the plan and the 3D view. A tee vertex moved off the grid can change a wall's length label from exact to approximate, or the reverse; the report counts such walls.
- The length formatting changes what most labels show (section 5.8, point 4). The input steps of point 5 land with it, so the labels move once.
- The length tolerance is derived from the number format, not from measured plans. If real projects hold junctions that are apart by more than 0.001 mm and less than 1 mm for a reason the migration does not see, rooms open. The join rule on input and the migration are the two guards, and the property tests probe the band between.
- Miters between a line and an arc, and between two arcs, are new geometry. They are the most likely place for a wrong corner. The corner property and a scene baseline at a tight radius are the guards.
- Moving the wall math from `engine/` to `core/` can move pixels by a rounding step. Each such pull request is checked against the baselines.
- The binding rule for room records has cases no table can list. A wrong binding puts a name on the wrong room, which a user sees at once and cannot explain. The property tests and the rule's fixed order are the guard. An unbound record is never deleted, so a wrong binding loses nothing.
- A name can come back on a new room (section 5.6, point 9).
- The room record step derives the rooms of a changed floor inside each wall command, on the network that the memo already holds (section 5.3, point 9). During an end drag that is one room derivation per pointer move, and the scene graph can derive the rooms part a second time after the step rewrote the records. No time was measured.
- An arc whose radius is near the bound of section 5.7 gives the thinnest inner face. A thickness change on an arc wall is refused when it would break the bound, which a user may not expect.
- Between pull request 15 and pull request 18 the arc path has no user. If the stage stops there, `main` carries code that only tests reach.
- Three lanes share `core/model/types.ts`, the migration folder, and the command handlers. The edits are small, but the command layer will rewrite the handlers while the kernel is still landing. The command layer specification takes this document's list as an input, together with the binding step of section 5.6, point 4.
- Other lanes publish revisions too: the view from the front plans two optional fields, and each version step of this stage starts a new revision count. A lane that takes a number another lane took first fails the schema drift check, which catches it before a merge.
- An older build at the same schema version draws an arc wall straight (section 5.7, point 6). The window is short and the build says a newer one saved the file.
- With the owner's answer on drawing over a wall (it merges), a user who draws over a wall to change it gets no change and one line of text.

## 11. Conflicts found

These are facts that do not match the umbrella specification, #687, or a decision record. None was worked around.

1. The umbrella's sixth kernel condition reads "A length typed in the wall tool reads back the same after save and reload, for every inch fraction down to 1/64 and every metric value down to 0.1 mm". ADR-0181 ("Consequences") names the wall tool too. The wall tool on `main` takes no typed length. It takes a point from a click, or from Enter at a keyboard candidate that the arrow keys move in 100 mm steps, and its other inputs are finish, backspace, and cancel (`editor/plan/wall-tool.ts:62-103`, `editor/plan/authoring-tool-handlers.ts:118-129`). No inspector field sets a wall's length either. The owner approved a Length field in the wall inspector (section 5.8, point 3), which tests the guarantee through the interface with every fraction and metric value of its range. He has not approved a change to the umbrella's wording. Open: the owner decides. This document proposes the wording "A length typed in the Length field of the wall inspector reads back the same after save and reload, for every inch fraction down to 1/64 and every metric value down to 0.1 mm", with the test through the wall tool moved to the stage for tools and input under an issue of its own (section 7), and ADR-0199 amending the consequence of ADR-0181 to match. That is the working assumption, recommended. The other options are to add typed length entry to the wall tool in this stage (one more pull request, which edits lists the stage for tools and input replaces), or to keep the wording and leave the condition open after this stage.
2. The umbrella and #687 list #675 among the issues the kernel settles. Pull request #794 closed #675 on 2026-11-16, and pull request #874 closed its follow-up, #774, on 2026-11-18. Neither is left for this stage.
3. The umbrella says caches use the change set of the command layer in place of object identity (its section 5.3). The kernel starts before the command layer, and its plan geometry can land before change sets exist, so its memo is keyed on object references (section 5.3, point 5). This is an order of work, and it is stated here so nobody reads the kernel as the final form.
4. The umbrella gives #676 to the command layer, with the condition that a cache keyed on the change set returns a fresh wall height (its section 7, "Commands"). Pull request #866 closed #676 on 2026-11-18 by keying the wall node on the floor id and the ceiling height. The issue is closed, but the condition is about the change set, so it stays the command layer's to meet.
5. The umbrella says 74 places read wall end points. On `main` today a search by name finds 82 lines with 129 reads in 25 production files, 2 of them inside the kernel, and reads under other names add at least 3 files (section 5.2). So at least 26 files outside the kernel read wall end points. It was not measured in full. This is why section 5.2 checks the condition by type.
6. The umbrella takes 1/256 inch as the smallest display step. On `main` the precision is a user preference with a default of 1/8 inch (`core/units/preferences.ts:18`), and this document did not find a control that limits it to 1/256. The tolerance is derived for 1/256 inch as the umbrella states it.
7. The umbrella orders the kernel after document identity and the command layer: "stop data loss, then document identity and the command layer, then each remaining area" (its section 6). The owner chose on 18 November to start this stage beside stage 2 (document identity) and before stage 3 (the command layer), and decision 5 puts the trial first. On 19 November he decided that the work that does not read or shape the plan geometry runs beside the trial. He did not decide that the kernel lands before stage 3. Open: the owner decides. The working assumption, recommended: the two stages land in the order of section 6 of this document, where neither waits for the other in the shared handler files. Section 6 of the umbrella gets a note when this document lands (section 8).
8. The umbrella cuts steps "so that two lanes do not edit the same files" (its section 3). This stage shares eight areas with the session layer lanes and a set of handler and editor files with the command layer, each by an agreed order (section 6). The shared files are listed so that the lanes can agree that order, but the umbrella's rule does not hold for them.
9. The binding step of section 5.6, point 4, runs inside every command that changes a floor's walls. It is registered beside the project commands in `core/commands/`, which ADR-0179 and the change sets of the command layer are about to redesign. The command layer specification takes it as an input, and when change sets exist the step becomes a reader of the change set.
10. ADR-0097 lists "the hole containment pass folding into faces" among the trial's criteria, and its context says that in a half-edge structure a hole "would not need a separate containment test". Every hole is a free-standing loop of walls, which is a separate component of the graph, and no half-edge links it to the face around it. So each hole needs one point-in-face test in any structure. Open: the owner decides how to read the criterion. The working assumption, recommended: it is met when holes come out of the build as inner cycles of faces, with at most one such test per free-standing component and no pass over rooms after the walk (section 5.3, the trial, measurement M4). Read strictly, no structure can meet it, and the trial would fail before it measures anything. ADR-0200 records the reading the owner chooses.
11. ADR-0097 sees most of a half-edge structure's value in one that persists and is updated in place. Section 5.3, point 3, rules that out, so the trial measures a structure rebuilt on each build (section 5.3, "What the trial can and cannot show").

What this document could not verify: the four cases of #681 were not run again, the cause given for #681 in section 5.5 and the stub wall case in section 5.6 are read from the code, the 3D result for an opening across a tee was not rendered, and no timing was measured.
